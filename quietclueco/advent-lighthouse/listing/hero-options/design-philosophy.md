# Lamplight Cartography

A design philosophy for the five hero images (written first, as the canvas-design skill asks).

**Warmth, measured.** The movement holds that warmth can be recorded with the patience of a surveyor. A glow is a
fact to be plotted, the way a chart plots a sounding or a keeper logs the hour the lamp was lit. Every composition
starts from one source of light (a window, a lamp, a wax seal, a page under a desk lamp) and arranges everything
else around it in quiet, deliberate distances. Nothing is decoration. Each mark should look as if it had been put
down by a meticulous hand that has done this ten thousand times.

**Space and form.** Large calm fields of a single material (paper, dark water, linen, wool) carry the composition.
Inside them, small objects repeat in strict rhythm: envelopes in a grid, soundings across a chart, snow falling in a
lattice, ruled lines on a page. The repetition is patient and exact, and the one break in it (the open envelope, the
lamp that stays dark, the underlined sentence) is where the eye lands. Edges stay clean, margins are generous, and
nothing touches the frame by accident. Every alignment is the product of painstaking adjustment.

**Colour and material.** The palette is short and intentional: cream paper, deep sea blue, rust red, moss, and the
ochre of lamplight, with one accent per image and never a second loud colour. Surfaces show their material, such as
paper fibre, gouache mottle, the weave of a cloth, a pencil's graphite sheen. The colour has to feel mixed by hand in
small batches and calibrated over many sittings.

**Type as an object.** Lettering is few words, set with craft: a confident serif or a tall condensed capital for the
name, a quiet tracked sans for the facts, a hand for the human note. Type sits in the scene like a printed label or
a chart cartouche, not pasted on top. It is sized to be read across a room and at a thumbnail, and it never crowds,
overlaps or crosses the image's safe edges. Typesetting at this level takes master-level patience.

**Hierarchy and rhythm.** One idea per image, stated by the picture. The headline adds what the picture cannot show
and never repeats it. The scale runs from one monumental element to many tiny systematic ones, so the image rewards
a two-second glance and a twenty-second look. The finished work should feel labored over, refined pass after pass,
until nothing can be taken away. It should be pristine, the obvious product of deep expertise.
