## What is inside (from listings and search snippets)

- **BrainMazeGames, Murder at the Villa:** set-up instructions, character roles and sheets, investigation questions;
  4–14 guests aged 16+, 40–180 minutes, host can play, works in person or online. Halloween night at a villa; the
  host of the party in the story is the victim.
- **MortalCase:** "freeplay, evidence-based mingle game", 16+, mixed genders. Host guide, character guides, evidence
  packs, action cards, party materials; a comparison chart shows what the essential game and the expansion add.
  Separate "DIY host kit for extra custom characters" listings ($2.26) and party props (play money).
- **FunPartyPeople:** "first-time host friendly, minimal prep, no acting required", 6 core characters plus expansion
  characters (one listing: 20 roles = 6 core + 14 expansion), team play for very large groups, editable
  invitation templates sold separately at $5. Bundles at $95–112.
- **Witch games (MurderMysteryGames and similar):** all-female casts are a strong niche ("hen party", "all female"
  in tags); 5–8 players, 2–4 hours, 4 rounds, expansion packs add 8 roles each.

## What reviewers praise and what they complain about

Praise (review snippets):
- "The instructions were clear and helpful"; "plenty of info and support for hosts and guests" (MortalCase).
- Buying the expansion made the game "10x more interesting" (MortalCase).
- Guests "got totally into their characters"; "hilarious and engaging" interaction (witch coven games).

Complaints seen across printable party kits (review round-ups and forums; no specific complaint text could be read for
the five listings above):
- Too much host prep and printing; boxed sets feel easier.
- Clues "not well incorporated": players could not tell how a clue was meant to be found or read.
- Wrong group size: a kit for 8 leaves guests without roles at 12; a kit for 20 feels hollow with 8.
- Too complex when side games run alongside the mystery.
- Generic stories with thin characters and little host guidance.

## The format we chose

1. **6–12 players: 6 core suspects + up to 6 extra roles.** Like FunPartyPeople and the MortalCase expansions, but
   in one listing. The game is solvable with only the 6 core roles; the extra roles add witnesses, secrets and fun,
   never a clue that the solution needs (checked by code).
2. **Host can play.** It leads the best-selling Halloween party listing (BrainMazeGames). Our host guide has no
   spoilers until a sealed section at the very end, and no booklet says who did it, not even the killer's, so the
   host can take a core role and solve it with everyone else.
3. **No acting, three rounds, 2–3 hours.** Each round: the host reads a short scene, everyone reads their booklet's
   round section, new clue cards go on the table, guests mingle and compare stories. Then the accusation sheet and the
   sealed solution.
4. **Clear evidence.** The complaint "clues not well incorporated" is the one our code checks can answer: every key
   clue is printed on a card or in a core booklet, the reading of every place word on the house plan is tested, and
   the timeline of every character is checked against every booklet.
5. **Everything for the party in one kit:** host guide, 12 booklets, 11 clue cards, invitations (print and phone),
   name badges, accusation sheets, a potion menu (with and without alcohol), awards, place cards.
6. **Price:** competitors charge $19–45 for a kit (the best seller $20.39). Our shop is at 50% off until 30 October.
   Suggestion: list price $28, sale price $14. Decision with Vladimir.
